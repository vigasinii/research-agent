# Space Telescopes

Space telescopes observe the universe from above Earth's atmosphere, which absorbs or distorts many wavelengths of light. Operating in space allows observation across the full electromagnetic spectrum and achieves much higher angular resolution than ground-based telescopes.

## Hubble Space Telescope

The Hubble Space Telescope was launched on April 24, 1990, aboard Space Shuttle Discovery. It has a 2.4-meter primary mirror and observes primarily in ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light. After launch, a flaw in the mirror's shape caused blurry images. NASA corrected the flaw during a servicing mission in December 1993 by installing corrective optics.

Hubble has made over 1.5 million observations and contributed to discoveries including the accelerating expansion of the universe, the age of the universe at approximately 13.8 billion years, and the atmospheric composition of exoplanets. The Hubble Deep Field images, which captured thousands of galaxies in a tiny patch of sky, fundamentally changed our understanding of the universe's scale.

## James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope launched on December 25, 2021, and reached its operational orbit at the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point (L2), approximately 1.5 million kilometers from Earth. Webb has a 6.5-meter segmented primary mirror composed of 18 gold-coated beryllium hexagonal segments. It observes primarily in the infrared, enabling it to see through dust clouds and observe the earliest galaxies that formed after the Big Bang.

Webb's first images, released July 12, 2022, included the deepest infrared image of the universe ever taken, showing galaxies as they appeared over 13 billion years ago. Webb has detected carbon dioxide in the atmosphere of exoplanet WASP-39b and observed active chemistry in star-forming nebulae.

The telescope is cooled to approximately minus 233 degrees Celsius using a large sunshield the size of a tennis court to block heat from the Sun, Earth, and Moon.

## Chandra X-ray Observatory

The Chandra X-ray Observatory, launched in 1999, observes X-rays from high-energy regions of the universe including black holes, neutron stars, and galaxy clusters. It has the highest angular resolution of any X-ray telescope. Chandra observations have mapped the distribution of dark matter in galaxy clusters and observed material spiraling into supermassive black holes.

## Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope

The Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope, launched in 2008, observes gamma rays, the highest-energy form of light. It has detected gamma-ray bursts, mapped the gamma-ray sky, and searched for signals from dark matter annihilation. Fermi discovered a population of millisecond pulsars and detected gamma rays from terrestrial lightning strikes called terrestrial gamma-ray flashes.
