# Solar System Overview

The solar system consists of the Sun and all objects gravitationally bound to it, including eight planets, five recognized dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, and countless smaller bodies including asteroids, comets, and Kuiper Belt objects.

## The Sun

The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star containing 99.86 percent of the solar system's total mass. It is composed primarily of hydrogen (73%) and helium (25%), with trace amounts of heavier elements. Energy is produced in the Sun's core by nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen to helium. The core temperature reaches about 15 million degrees Celsius.

The Sun's surface, or photosphere, has a temperature of approximately 5,500 degrees Celsius. The corona, the Sun's outer atmosphere, paradoxically reaches temperatures of one to three million degrees Celsius. The mechanism heating the corona remains an active area of research.

## The Inner Planets

Mercury is the smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Despite its proximity to the Sun, Mercury's lack of atmosphere means its dark side reaches minus 180 degrees Celsius. Its surface is heavily cratered.

Venus has a thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide creating a greenhouse effect that raises surface temperature to about 465 degrees Celsius, hotter than Mercury. Atmospheric pressure on Venus is 93 times Earth's, equivalent to being 900 meters underwater.

Earth is the only known planet with life. It has liquid water on its surface, a protective magnetic field, and a nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere.

Mars is covered by iron oxide dust giving it a reddish color. It has the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, standing 21.9 kilometers high, and the longest canyon, Valles Marineris, stretching 4,000 kilometers.

## The Outer Planets

Jupiter is the largest planet, with a mass 318 times Earth's. Its Great Red Spot is a storm that has persisted for at least 350 years. Jupiter has at least 95 known moons. Europa, one of Jupiter's moons, is thought to harbor a subsurface liquid ocean beneath its icy crust.

Saturn's ring system is its most distinctive feature, composed primarily of ice particles and rocky debris. Saturn's rings extend up to 282,000 kilometers from the planet but are remarkably thin, averaging about 10 meters in depth.

Uranus rotates on its side with an axial tilt of 98 degrees. Its atmosphere contains methane, which gives it a blue-green color. Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope, in 1781 by William Herschel.

Neptune is the windiest planet, with wind speeds reaching 2,100 kilometers per hour. Its largest moon, Triton, orbits in the opposite direction of Neptune's rotation, suggesting it was captured from the Kuiper Belt.
