# Rocket Propulsion

Rockets work on the principle of Newton's third law of motion: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. By expelling mass at high velocity in one direction, a rocket generates thrust in the opposite direction. This principle works in the vacuum of space, where there is no air to push against.

## Chemical Propulsion

The vast majority of rockets use chemical propulsion, burning propellant to produce hot exhaust gases. The effectiveness of a rocket engine is measured by specific impulse, which represents the thrust produced per unit of propellant consumed per second. Higher specific impulse means better efficiency.

Liquid propellant rockets use separate tanks for fuel and oxidizer that combine in a combustion chamber. Liquid oxygen and liquid hydrogen produce the highest specific impulse of common propellant combinations, approximately 450 seconds in vacuum. The Space Shuttle Main Engines and the upper stage of Saturn V used this combination.

The Saturn V rocket that carried Apollo astronauts to the Moon remains the most powerful rocket ever flown. Its first stage produced 34 million newtons of thrust by burning a mixture of refined kerosene (RP-1) and liquid oxygen. The Saturn V could deliver 130 metric tons to low Earth orbit.

SpaceX's Falcon 9 uses liquid oxygen and RP-1, the same propellant combination as Saturn V's first stage. Its Merlin engines achieve a vacuum specific impulse of 311 seconds. The Falcon 9 pioneered the recovery of orbital-class rocket boosters by landing them vertically after stage separation, dramatically reducing launch costs.

SpaceX's Starship uses liquid oxygen and liquid methane propellants. The choice of methane is partly motivated by the possibility of producing it on Mars from atmospheric carbon dioxide and subsurface water through the Sabatier reaction, enabling in-situ resource utilization.

## Ion Propulsion

Ion thrusters accelerate electrically charged atoms (ions) using electric fields to produce thrust. They have very high specific impulse, up to 10,000 seconds, but produce very low thrust. They are efficient for long-duration deep space missions where time is not critical.

NASA's Dawn spacecraft used ion propulsion to orbit both Vesta and Ceres in the asteroid belt, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit two extraterrestrial bodies. The Hayabusa2 mission used ion engines to travel to asteroid Ryugu and return samples to Earth.

## Nuclear Propulsion

Nuclear thermal propulsion uses a nuclear reactor to heat propellant, which then exits through a nozzle to produce thrust. Specific impulse is roughly twice that of chemical rockets. The United States tested nuclear thermal rockets in the NERVA program from 1955 to 1972 but never flew them in space. NASA and DARPA have funded development of a nuclear thermal propulsion demonstration mission targeting the late 2020s.
