# Space Stations

Space stations are large spacecraft that remain in orbit for extended periods and are designed for humans to live and work aboard. They enable long-duration spaceflight research and serve as waypoints for further exploration.

## Salyut and Mir

The Soviet Union launched the first space station, Salyut 1, in April 1971. The Soyuz 11 crew lived aboard for 23 days, a record at the time, but tragically died during reentry when a faulty valve caused the capsule to depressurize. Six more Salyut stations followed.

Mir, launched in 1986, was the first modular space station, assembled from multiple components launched separately. Mir remained inhabited continuously for nearly a decade and set records for long-duration spaceflight. Cosmonaut Valeri Polyakov spent 437 consecutive days aboard Mir from 1994 to 1995, still the record for the longest single spaceflight. Mir was deorbited in 2001 after 15 years in operation.

## International Space Station

The International Space Station began construction in 1998 with the launch of the Russian Zarya module. The first long-duration crew arrived on November 2, 2000, beginning a continuous human presence that has lasted over 24 years. The ISS has a total mass of approximately 420,000 kilograms and a pressurized volume of 916 cubic meters, comparable to a six-bedroom house.

Scientific research aboard the ISS covers biology, physics, astronomy, meteorology, and other fields. Research in microgravity has led to advances in understanding bone density loss, muscle atrophy, and fluid dynamics. The ISS is visible from Earth with the naked eye as a bright moving dot, traveling across the sky in roughly four minutes.

The ISS is funded and operated by 15 nations. NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA are the primary partners. Planning is underway for transition to commercial space stations in the late 2020s and early 2030s as the ISS is retired.

## Chinese Space Station

China's Tiangong space station began construction with the launch of its core module, Tianhe, in April 2021. Two laboratory modules, Wentian and Mengtian, were added in 2022. The completed station has a mass of approximately 90,000 kilograms. China has conducted multiple crewed missions to Tiangong and operates it independently. The station is designed for a 10-year operational life.
