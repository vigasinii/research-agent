# History of Space Exploration

Space exploration began in earnest during the mid-twentieth century, driven by competition between the United States and the Soviet Union during the Cold War. This era, commonly called the Space Race, produced some of the most remarkable technological achievements in human history.

## The Soviet Early Lead

The Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1 on October 4, 1957, becoming the first nation to place an artificial satellite in Earth orbit. Sputnik 1 was a 58-centimeter aluminum sphere weighing 83.6 kilograms. It transmitted radio signals for 21 days before its batteries died and continued orbiting until January 4, 1958.

Yuri Gagarin became the first human to travel to space on April 12, 1961. His Vostok 1 spacecraft completed one orbit of Earth in 108 minutes. Gagarin reported that Earth appeared strikingly blue from orbit, a description that has become iconic in space exploration history.

## The Apollo Program

The United States responded to Soviet achievements with President John F. Kennedy's 1961 declaration that America would land a man on the Moon before the end of the decade. NASA's Apollo program, which employed over 400,000 people at its peak, achieved this goal on July 20, 1969, when Apollo 11 astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed on the lunar surface.

Armstrong's first words upon stepping onto the Moon were: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." The Apollo program conducted six successful lunar landings between 1969 and 1972, with the last mission being Apollo 17 in December 1972. Apollo 17 commander Eugene Cernan was the last human to walk on the Moon.

## The Space Shuttle Era

NASA's Space Shuttle program ran from 1981 to 2011, conducting 135 missions. The shuttle was the first reusable orbital spacecraft. The program suffered two catastrophic failures: Challenger disintegrated 73 seconds after launch on January 28, 1986, killing all seven crew members; Columbia broke apart during reentry on February 1, 2003, also killing all seven crew members.

Despite these tragedies, the Space Shuttle program built the International Space Station, launched the Hubble Space Telescope, and demonstrated that humans could work routinely in space.

## The International Space Station

The International Space Station (ISS) has been continuously inhabited since November 2000. It orbits Earth at an average altitude of approximately 400 kilometers, traveling at 7.7 kilometers per second and completing 15.5 orbits per day. The ISS is a joint project of NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA. It serves as a microgravity laboratory for research in biology, physics, astronomy, and other fields.
