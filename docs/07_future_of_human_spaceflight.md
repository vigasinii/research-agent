# Future of Human Spaceflight

Human spaceflight is entering a new era characterized by commercial involvement, renewed lunar ambitions, and long-term planning for Mars.

## Artemis Program

NASA's Artemis program aims to return humans to the Moon by the late 2020s. Unlike Apollo, which used expendable rockets and capsules, Artemis uses the Space Launch System (SLS), a heavy-lift rocket, combined with the Orion crew capsule and commercial lunar landers. The program aims to establish a sustainable human presence on and around the Moon, including the Lunar Gateway, a small space station in lunar orbit.

Artemis I, an uncrewed test flight, launched on November 16, 2022. The mission sent the Orion capsule around the Moon and back, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean on December 11, 2022. Artemis II will carry astronauts on a similar trajectory, and Artemis III aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the lunar surface.

A key goal of the Artemis program is demonstrating technologies and operations needed for eventual crewed Mars missions, including life support, navigation, and resource utilization.

## Commercial Crew and Cargo

SpaceX's Dragon spacecraft has transported astronauts to the International Space Station since 2020 under NASA's Commercial Crew Program, ending American dependence on Russian Soyuz spacecraft. Boeing is developing the Starliner capsule for the same purpose. Commercial cargo missions from SpaceX and Northrop Grumman regularly resupply the ISS.

SpaceX is developing Starship as an all-purpose heavy-lift vehicle capable of carrying 100 or more people to the Moon or Mars. Starship uses full flow staged combustion engines burning liquid methane and liquid oxygen. It is designed to be fully reusable, with both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage landing for reuse. SpaceX aims to use Starship as NASA's Human Landing System for Artemis III.

## Mars Human Mission Concepts

Multiple organizations have studied crewed Mars mission architectures. Key challenges include radiation exposure during the 6-to-9 month transit, long communication delays (up to 24 minutes one way), life support for surface operations, and landing large masses in Mars's thin atmosphere.

SpaceX has described a long-term vision of establishing a self-sustaining city on Mars with a population of one million people. Elon Musk has suggested that such a city would require approximately 1,000 Starship flights carrying crew and cargo.

NASA's Mars mission concepts focus on a phased approach beginning with lunar operations, followed by deep space habitation testing, and eventually crewed Mars orbit and surface missions in the 2030s or 2040s.
