# Mars Missions

Mars has been a primary target for robotic exploration since the 1960s. Its relative proximity, day length similar to Earth's, and evidence of ancient water make it the most studied planet beyond Earth.

## Early Missions

The first successful Mars flyby was Mariner 4 in July 1965, which returned 21 grainy photographs of the Martian surface. These images revealed a cratered, seemingly dead world, disappointing scientists who had hoped for signs of life. Mariner 9, launched in 1971, became the first spacecraft to orbit another planet. It mapped 85 percent of the Martian surface and discovered Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system.

## Viking Landers

NASA's Viking program in 1976 placed two landers on the Martian surface. Each lander carried experiments designed to detect life. The results were ambiguous and remain debated, but no definitive evidence of life was found. The Viking landers measured Martian atmospheric pressure at about 600 pascals, roughly 0.6 percent of Earth's atmospheric pressure at sea level.

## Mars Rovers

The Mars Pathfinder mission in 1997 deployed the Sojourner rover, demonstrating that mobile science platforms could operate on Mars. Sojourner operated for 83 days, far exceeding its design life of 7 days.

NASA's Mars Exploration Rovers, Spirit and Opportunity, landed in 2004. Opportunity operated for nearly 15 years, traversing 45 kilometers before a global dust storm silenced it in June 2018. The rovers confirmed that liquid water once existed on Mars by finding sedimentary rocks formed in aqueous environments.

The Curiosity rover landed in Gale Crater in August 2012. Using the Sample Analysis at Mars instrument suite, Curiosity detected organic molecules in Martian rock and confirmed that ancient Mars had conditions suitable for microbial life.

The Perseverance rover landed in Jezero Crater on February 18, 2021. Perseverance carries the Ingenuity helicopter, which completed the first powered flight on another planet on April 19, 2021. Perseverance is collecting rock cores for potential future return to Earth.

## Mars Atmosphere

Mars has a thin atmosphere composed primarily of carbon dioxide (95.3%), with nitrogen (2.7%), argon (1.6%), and trace amounts of other gases including oxygen (0.13%) and water vapor (0.03%). The Martian atmosphere is too thin to protect the surface from ultraviolet radiation and provides insufficient pressure for liquid water to exist stably on the surface today.

Average surface temperature on Mars is about minus 60 degrees Celsius, ranging from minus 125 degrees Celsius at the poles in winter to 20 degrees Celsius at the equator during summer.
