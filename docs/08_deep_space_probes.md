# Deep Space Probes

Robotic probes have traveled to every planet in the solar system and beyond, providing detailed data about environments too distant or hostile for human visitors.

## Voyager Program

The twin Voyager spacecraft, launched in 1977, took advantage of a rare planetary alignment that allowed a single trajectory to visit multiple outer planets. Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980, then was redirected toward interstellar space. Voyager 2 flew past Jupiter in 1979, Saturn in 1981, Uranus in 1986, and Neptune in 1989. It remains the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus and Neptune.

Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the boundary between the solar wind and interstellar space, in August 2012, becoming the first human-made object to enter interstellar space. As of 2024, Voyager 1 is approximately 23 billion kilometers from the Sun, communicating with Earth via signals that take over 22 hours to arrive.

Voyager 2 entered interstellar space in November 2018. Both spacecraft continue to transmit scientific data, though their power from radioisotope thermoelectric generators is declining and instruments are being switched off to extend mission life.

## New Horizons

The New Horizons spacecraft launched in January 2006 and reached Pluto on July 14, 2015, returning the first detailed images of Pluto and its moons. Images revealed that Pluto has a nitrogen ice plain called Sputnik Planitia in the shape of a heart, mountains of water ice, and surprisingly complex geology indicating ongoing geological activity.

After the Pluto flyby, New Horizons flew past Arrokoth (formerly Ultima Thule), a Kuiper Belt object, on January 1, 2019. Arrokoth is a contact binary, two lobes joined together, indicating it formed through gentle collision of two objects orbiting in the same direction.

## Cassini-Huygens

The Cassini spacecraft orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017, conducting detailed study of Saturn, its rings, and its moons. The Huygens probe, carried by Cassini, descended through the atmosphere of Saturn's moon Titan in January 2005, landing on its surface and returning data about Titan's methane rain and lakes.

Cassini discovered active geysers at the south pole of Enceladus, Saturn's icy moon, jetting water vapor and ice particles into space. Analysis of the plume material suggested that Enceladus has a subsurface liquid water ocean with hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor, making it one of the most promising locations in the solar system to search for life. Cassini intentionally plunged into Saturn's atmosphere in September 2017 to prevent biological contamination of Enceladus.
